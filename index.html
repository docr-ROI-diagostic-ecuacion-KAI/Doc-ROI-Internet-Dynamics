FORMATIVE CONSULTATION 05 · Laboratory
SESSION 1

Understanding communication: the process behind Internet Dynamics.

Learning objective

Before modelling Internet, we must understand communication. A communication process is not simply the transmission of a message. It is a structured exchange in which an emitter encodes meaning, selects a mode and a medium, sends a message, reaches a receiver, and receives feedback that changes the next interaction.

The decisive point is feedback. Without feedback, the process remains information. With feedback, the system becomes communication, because the receiver is no longer passive: the receiver answers, reacts, accepts, rejects, delays, shares, buys, complains, subscribes, leaves a trace or creates a new signal.
Methodological rule

First map the communication process. Then identify which element of the process has been digitalized, measured or automated. Only after that can Internet Dynamics be modelled as a system of users, content and infrastructure.

Translation into Internet Dynamics: users act as emitters and receivers; content carries the message, offer and meaning; infrastructure provides the channel, encoding, decoding, measurement and data capture. Feedback becomes data, and data allows the organization to manage the next communication decision.

EmitterWho starts the signal: company, customer, platform, device or AI agent.
MessageThe offer, content, brand promise, service, product or response being exchanged.
Medium / ChannelThe technical and relational path: web, email, search, app, marketplace, workflow AI or agent protocol.
FeedbackThe measurable answer: click, lead, conversion, churn, review, ticket, purchase, delay or satisfaction signal.

Key concepts: communication process · feedback loop · emitter · receiver · customer · noise · message · medium · data trace.

Sources: Shannon and Weaver (1949), Wiener (1948), Schramm (1954), Jakobson (1960), Watzlawick, Beavin and Jackson (1967), Forrester (1961).

SESSION 2

Data gives access to information and turns feedback into relationship capacity.

Learning objective

Understand the second layer of Internet Dynamics: once the communication process exists, the organization needs access to the information generated by that process. Data is not an accessory. Data is the operational evidence that allows the organization to know whether the message arrived, whether the receiver understood it, whether the channel worked, and whether the next interaction should change.

If there is no data, there is no managed communication. Without data, the organization cannot interpret the receiver, cannot personalize the message, cannot learn from feedback and cannot build a stable relationship. The process may emit content, but it cannot govern the relationship created by Internet.
Methodological rule

Every element of the communication process must be translated into a measurable digital trace: user signal, content interaction, infrastructure event, ecosystem condition and feedback response. That trace is the bridge between communication theory and system dynamics.

How the second layer works: users create and receive signals; content expresses the message and the offer; infrastructure encodes, delivers, measures and decodes the interaction; the ecosystem introduces noise, friction and opportunities; the data loop returns the result to management so the next message can be more accurate.

User signalAudience, customer, lead, cohort, connected object or AI agent response.
Content signalSearch intent, keyword, click, view, conversion, rejection, review or consumption pattern.
Infrastructure signalChannel, device, CRM, ERP, analytics, tag, workflow AI, performance or transaction event.
Data loopThe feedback that returns to the organization as information, intelligence and next action.

Key concepts: data access · signal capture · feedback evidence · relationship capacity · content interaction · infrastructure event · ecosystem noise · managed communication.

Sources: Wiener (1948), Shannon and Weaver (1949), Forrester (1961), Luhmann (1995), Sterman (2000), Meadows (2008).

SESSION 3

Forrester gives us the language: stocks, flows, feedback, delays and reinforcing loops.

Learning objective

Understand Internet as a dynamic system, not as a list of digital channels. A system is a set of related elements that interact to produce a common purpose. Forrester's key contribution is that a system can be expressed mathematically through levels that accumulate, flows that change those levels, and feedback that modifies future flows.

In Internet Dynamics, the communication process is transferred into system entities. Users are the emitter and receiver side of the relationship. Content is the message, offer and mode. Infrastructure is the medium, coding and decoding capacity. Data is the feedback memory of the system. The ecosystem introduces noise, friction and acceleration.
Methodological rule

The laboratory applies this logic in a simple way: it treats Users, Content and Infrastructure as levels, calculates their normalized state, applies inflows and outflows as changes over time, and then adjusts the result with management, exogenous conditions and AI acceleration.

Transfer from communication to system dynamics: the emitter/receiver relation becomes the user stock; the message becomes the content stock; the medium and coding/decoding capacity become infrastructure; feedback becomes data; noise becomes exogenous pressure; AI becomes an acceleration factor when the organization can act on the feedback.

StocksUsers, content and infrastructure accumulate and define the current state of the system.
FlowsAcquisition, production, investment, consumption, churn, obsolescence and friction change the stocks.
FeedbackData returns from the receiver and modifies the following content, channel or relationship decision.
DelayThe effect of a campaign, platform, process, system or AI automation rarely appears instantly.
Forrester stock-and-flow formulation Level(t) = Level(t0) + integral[Inflow(t) - Outflow(t)] dt
dLevel/dt = Inflow(t) - Outflow(t)This is the continuous form. The level changes because something enters the system and something leaves it. If inflow is greater than outflow, the level grows. If outflow is greater than inflow, the level decreases.
Level(t+Delta t) = Level(t) + (Inflow - Outflow) x Delta tThis is the discrete version used by the laboratory. The simulator advances step by step: month by month, week by week or period by period.
Users(t+1), Content(t+1), Infrastructure(t+1)In Internet Dynamics, the main levels are Users, Content and Infrastructure. Each one grows through inflows such as acquisition, production, investment or AI automation, and decreases through outflows such as churn, saturation, obsolescence, cost or friction.

Key concepts: dynamic system · stock · flow · reinforcing loop · delay · feedback · exogenous friction · AI acceleration.

Sources: Forrester (1961), Bertalanffy (1968), Sterman (2000), Meadows (2008), Wiener (1948), Shannon and Weaver (1949).

SESSION 4

The model has three endogenous nodes and two management layers.

Learning objective

Understand the operating architecture of Internet Dynamics. The model is deliberately simple at the centre: Users, Content and Infrastructure. Everything else is connected as a subnode, measure, weight, friction or acceleration factor. This keeps the system teachable, measurable and usable inside the laboratory.

Methodological language matters. In this capsule we use management. A digital communication system is not a closed machine; it is a living relationship system that can be influenced, accelerated, slowed down, measured and improved through processes, organization, systems, data, AI and exogenous conditions.
Users - UPotential revenue and relationship

Audience, cookies, cohorts, leads, fans, clients, promoters, IoT devices and AI agents. Users represent the market side of communication.

Content - COffer, message and cost

SEO, SEM, paid media, social content, email, web, product, service, brand, keywords and consumption signals. Content carries value and also creates communication cost.

Infrastructure - IMedium, channel and technical capacity

Devices, channels, workflow AI, CRM, ERP, analytics, tags, payment, ecommerce, cloud, performance and data capture. Infrastructure enables the loop and also consumes resources.

Company managementProcesses, organization, systems and data/AI

This layer determines whether the organization can manage the communication system, personalize interactions and act on feedback.

Exogenous managementLegal, market and cultural pressure

PESTEL, GDPR, competition, innovation, economic pressure and market culture can accelerate or slow the system.

AI accelerationAgents, automation and personalization

AI increases system speed when data, workflows and business rules allow the organization to respond at the right time, through the right channel, with the right content.

Feedback Loop

Feedback is the final condition for digital communication to exist as a managed system. It is produced when the buyer persona interacts with content through infrastructure and leaves evidence: clicks, access, searches, forms, purchases, service requests, satisfaction signals, recurrent behaviour or data generated by AI agents. That evidence returns to management and allows the organization to improve the next communication decision.

EvidenceFeedback transforms behaviour into data that can be measured, interpreted and compared over time.
RelationshipFeedback shows whether the message, channel and offer are aligned with the buyer persona.
ActionFeedback activates new decisions in processes, systems, data/AI and personalized communication.

Key concepts: endogenous variables · management layers · subnodes · weights · feedback loop · relationship signals · AI acceleration · measurable communication.

Sources: Forrester (1961), Sterman (2000), Meadows (2008), Porter (1985), Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan (2021), Davenport and Ronanki (2018).
